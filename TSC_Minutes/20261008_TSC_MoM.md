## 1. LF Energy Graduated Project status and OpenSSF Gold
SEAPATH has been approved for LF Energy Graduated status. Completion of the OpenSSF Gold badge remains an outstanding objective. Two main technical areas were discussed:
- Reproducible builds: discussions continue with maintainers and LF Energy, with SUSE and Red Hat invited. The scope of the requirement, including installer and CI-generated binaries, still needs clarification.
- Automated tests: coverage was added or improved for vm_manager, python3-setup-ovs and custom Ansible-related scripts/plugins.

## 2. vIED / vendor integration process
The group discussed how virtual IED and application vendors should integrate with SEAPATH, covering documentation, technical configuration and release compatibility.
- Vendor-specific documentation should remain maintained by the vendor; SEAPATH Wiki may reference it and provide concise integration guidance rather than duplicating it.
- Vendors are encouraged to reuse the existing Ansible/libvirt template and contribute missing capabilities upstream rather than introducing separate templates.
- Conclusion: vendors may contribute example Ansible inventories to the SEAPATH codebase, but remain responsible for maintaining and validating them against SEAPATH versions. The integration guidance should be documented on the SEAPATH Wiki.
- SEAPATH releases should not be driven by individual vendor integrations; SEAPATH does not certify or comprehensively test every third-party application. Marketing claims and technical compatibility should be kept distinct.

## 3. Roadmap management and community engagement
The roadmap was reviewed during the SEAPATH meetup on 17 September in Berlin. Klaxoon was useful for initial collaborative planning but does not adequately track statuses and pull requests. GitHub issues in the seapath/.github repository are now the preferred tracking mechanism.
- Each roadmap item should have an identified leader, who coordinates meetings, shares minutes with the community and TSC/PMO, creates and assigns GitHub issues, escalates blockers and reports relevant outcomes or demonstrations to the TSC.
- GitHub issues and pull requests will be used to track progress and dependencies; contributors are encouraged to flag missing access, reviews or other blockers.

## 4. Active roadmap items
- TSC evolution and governance: several issues and pull requests are under discussion; a progress update is expected at a subsequent TSC meeting.
- Demonstration platform and sandbox: a draft SEAPATH introduction video has been produced by RTE International; feedback and a TSC update are still needed.
- Documentation as code: work is underway to evaluate Read the Docs and the seapath/docs repository. A proof of concept should be presented to the TSC, potentially in November or December. The existing Confluence content would be migrated only after validation.
- Supervision using Prometheus: one pull request merged; another approved and awaiting CI resolution/merge.
- ISO generation helper tool: a proof of concept exists, but the community should discuss whether a shared tool is preferable to vendor-specific tools. Florent proposed a dedicated discussion with interested contributors.
- Running containers alongside SEAPATH: initial support was reported as implemented; further integration and discussion are needed.
- Resource allocation / VM XML: an RFC was accepted for discussion by maintainers; technical review remains outstanding.
- Guidelines for virtual-function development: no new progress reported; follow-up with item leaders is needed.

## 5. Vote — SEAPATH acronym
Florent (RTE) proposed changing the expansion of SEAPATH to “Secure Edge Automation Platform and Trusted Hypervisor”, replacing “Software Enabled Automation Platform and Artifacts Therein”.
Decision: APPROVED — four votes in favor out of five TSC voting members; one member absent. The four favorable votes included one submitted by email before the meeting. Official documentation and communication materials should be updated accordingly.
